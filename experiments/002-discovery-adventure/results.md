# Experiment 002: Discovery vs. adventure boundaries — Results

## Status

**Result:** Strong support for the revised discovery hypotheses. No new adventure hypothesis is justified yet.

Experiment 002 used the revised H01-H09 model from Experiment 001 without further changes before prediction. The holdout set was designed to test two specific questions:

1. Does the user respond differently when discovery is the main activity versus when discovery is supported by narrative structure, goals, stakes, or other systems?
2. Is there enough evidence for a new independent preference around straightforward adventure, charisma, momentum, or spectacle?

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Witness | mixed | mixed | exact |
| The Forgotten City | positive | positive | exact |

**Summary:** 6 exact matches, 2 one-step intensity near misses, 0 polarity misses. All 8 predictions got the positive/mixed direction right.

## Discovery boundary

This was the clearest result.

### The Witness
Prediction: mixed  
Actual: mixed

This supports the revised H01/H07 caution around experiences where observation, deduction, and discovery are unusually central and external narrative propulsion is sparse.

### The Forgotten City
Prediction: positive  
Actual: positive

This supports the idea that mystery/discovery can work well when attached to explicit goals, dialogue, consequences, and a strong narrative problem.

### Firewatch
Prediction: positive  
Actual: positive

This supports the same distinction from another direction: environmental exploration can function well when it primarily serves character and narrative rather than asking exploration itself to carry the experience.

### 999
Prediction: positive  
Actual: strong_positive

This is the only intensity underestimate in the discovery group. It strengthens the case that mystery plus clear goals, stakes, puzzles, and strong narrative propulsion is a particularly favorable combination.

## Adventure boundary

The results do **not** currently justify a separate adventure hypothesis.

### The Mummy
Prediction: positive  
Actual: positive

The existing H02/H05/H09 model was sufficient.

### National Treasure
Prediction: positive  
Actual: positive

This was one of the cleanest tests for a missing adventure axis. Because the existing model predicted it correctly, there is no evidence here that straightforward adventure/momentum requires its own hypothesis.

### Pirates of the Caribbean
Prediction: strong_positive  
Actual: positive

The model slightly overpredicted rather than underpredicted. That weakens the case that adventure itself is being systematically undervalued.

### Mad Max: Fury Road
Prediction: strong_positive  
Actual: strong_positive

H02/H08 already explained this result well through distinctive execution and physical spectacle.

## Interpretation

The *Last Crusade* underprediction from Experiment 001 should remain an unresolved item rather than being used to create a new broad preference.

Experiment 002 suggests that:
- the user's adventure favorites may often already be explained by other preferences;
- not every charismatic adventure should be promoted to strongest-tier;
- more evidence would be needed before adding a durable "straightforward adventure" hypothesis.

## Model implications

### H01
The revised version gained support. Discovery works better as a conditional signal when paired with structure, stakes, goals, character, or other established positives.

### H07
The revised version gained strong support. Exploration appears better modeled as an enhancer than a primary driver.

### H09
Receives some support from 999 and The Mummy, but the experiment does not isolate it strongly enough to justify a major change.

### H02/H08
Mad Max: Fury Road supports the current idea that distinctive execution and exceptional physical spectacle can independently drive a strong prediction.

## Conclusion

Experiment 002 successfully narrowed one important taste boundary:

> **Discovery is much more predictive when it is serving a structured experience than when discovery itself is expected to generate most of the user's momentum.**

The experiment did **not** establish a separate adventure/charisma/spectacle preference. The current model should preserve that as an open question rather than adding a new hypothesis.
